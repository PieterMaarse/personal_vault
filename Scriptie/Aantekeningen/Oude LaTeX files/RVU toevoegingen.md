To partly alleviate this issue, a new early retirement policy was introduced in 2021, in the form of the RVU levy exemption. This regulation offers the possibility of fiscal benefits when retiring up to 3 years before the SRA, and is aimed at supporting employees who are not able to reach the SRA in good health due to heaviness of their work \citep{minszenw2025handreiking}.


Since it was deemed impossible to determine on a central level what is heavy work (wijziging pensioenwet), the exemption was deployed as a general tax measure and it was left up to employers and employees to determine who can use an RVU, either through a collective or an individual agreement. As a result, it is possible that the exemption is used by employees with non-demanding work for whom it is not intended. \citet{minSZW2025RVUcao} investigate this efficiency and show that in January 2025, not all RVU agreements in caos are restricted to employees with demanding work, and almost none are defined based on objective criteria.

Originally, the exemption was scheduled to run until the end of 2025, but following the agreement "Gezond naar het Pensioen", it will be continued from 2026 onward with the aim to become structural \citep{gezond2025uitwerking}. The exemption will stay a general tax measure, but continuation is under the prerequisite that RVUs will be used more targeted and controlled during cao negotiations \citep{minszenw2025handreiking}. To this end, TNO has developed an assessment framework to more objectively classify what jobs are demanding \citep{TNO2025toetsing}, and using this framework they will validate whether the descriptions of RVU-eligible functions in caos meet the proposed criteria. Individual arrangements are not evaluated but parties involved are encouraged to follow the guidelines \citep{gezond2025uitwerking}.

Even though employers bear the main financial burden of an RVU, misuse of the exemption can cost the public large sums of money (toevoegen). Whereas it is important to properly evaluate the efficiency of the exemption, especially with the recently proposed increases in the SRA, research into its efficiency is limited.

Past research investigates the efficiency of the exemption by researching RVU possibilities in collective agreements and infer from this how many employees are eligible \citep{minSZW2025RVUcao}. Additionally, official documents state that RVUs must be applied targeted and controlled \textit{during cao nogations} \citep{minszenw2025handreiking}, and TNO will evaluate function descriptions in caos \citep{TNO2025toetsing}. We argue, however, that actual usage should be considered in order to evaluate the efficiency of the exemption properly, and that individually-agreed RVUs should also be taken into account.
